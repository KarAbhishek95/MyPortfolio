About Me

I'm a Mechanical Product Design Engineer with 4+ years of experience designing and building hardware from concept through production. My work spans precision opto-mechanical and electromechanical systems for semiconductor equipment, instrumentation, structural and thermal analysis, and reliability engineering for production machinery. I own designs end to end—from requirements and CAD through GD&T, tolerance analysis, FEA/CFD, prototyping, validation, DFM, and manufacturing.

My favorite kind of problem is one where the obvious answer is wrong. On one program, precision assemblies repeatedly failed alignment even though every component was within specification. Rather than tightening tolerances or re-machining parts, I used statistical tolerance analysis to trace the real problem to the datum scheme, redesigned the referencing strategy, and reduced alignment-related build issues by 25%.

That experience reflects how I approach engineering: understand the physics, follow the data, find the true constraint, and make the solution work in hardware. I'm most interested in building high-performance products where precision, manufacturability, and reliability are non-negotiable, turning complex engineering problems into robust, production-ready designs.

Featured Engineering Projects

Design and analysis, worked end to end

Two graduate projects that show how I move from first principles to a validated result: a single-degree-of-freedom mechanism synthesized from scratch and built as a prototype, and a computational fluid dynamics solver written from the governing equations up.

Mechanism Design · Kinematic Synthesis

Single-Stage Variable Rocket Nozzle: Six-Bar Linkage

MATLAB · LINCAGES · Type Synthesis

Designed a mechanism that continuously varies a rocket nozzle's exit diameter from 0.9 m at sea level to 3.3 m in vacuum, so a single stage can stay near optimal expansion across the whole flight and be recovered and reused. The result is a Stephenson III six-bar linkage, selected and dimensioned through full kinematic synthesis and validated with a physical prototype.

The problem

A fixed nozzle is optimized for one altitude, so it runs over-expanded near the ground and under-expanded high up, wasting thrust. That is a major reason second stages are usually discarded rather than recovered. The mechanism had to vary the nozzle profile continuously, keep all ground links inside the rocket body, and hold a transmission angle above 30 degrees so it never binds or locks.

Type synthesis

I started with type synthesis rather than jumping to a shape. Using the Grubler and Kutzbach criterion with one degree of freedom and six links, I found the mechanism needed seven lower-pair joints and two independent loops. Enumerating the valid link sets gave the Stephenson and Watt chains and their inversions, eight candidate topologies in total.

Down-selection

I screened the candidates against the real constraints: the ground had to be a ternary link for a sturdy mount, the mechanism had to be far-reaching for motion generation, and the transmission angle had to stay above 30 degrees. That eliminated Stephenson I, Stephenson II, and Watt I, leaving Watt II and Stephenson III for detailed analysis.

Precision points

I set the precision points from the nozzle geometry itself, using the Pythagorean theorem on the sea-level and vacuum diameters and the fixed 0.226 m throat to get the required outer-nozzle lengths, then placed three positions the coupler had to pass through.

Analytical + graphical synthesis

I solved the mechanism two ways for confidence: a graphical MK-circle construction, and an analytical complex-number loop-closure solved in MATLAB (setting up the standard dyad equations A·x = B for the W and Z vectors). LINCAGES was used to explore the solution space and confirm the pivots stayed inside the rocket body.

Result

Stephenson III won. It held a transmission angle above 30 degrees with consistent mechanical advantage and angular velocity through all three precision positions, so it drives the nozzle smoothly without binding. Watt II failed to reach the prescribed positions. I then built a physical prototype to confirm the motion.

Computational Fluid Dynamics · Numerical Methods

Computational Modelling of a 2D Mixing Chamber

Navier-Stokes · MATLAB · Finite Difference

Built a computational fluid dynamics solver from the governing equations up to study transient mixing in a two-dimensional chamber with four inlets and two outlets that open and close on a schedule, tracking both the velocity field and the concentration of a chemical species over time at Reynolds number 100 and Schmidt number 2.

The problem

Model unsteady, incompressible flow and species transport in a square chamber where the inlets switch their species on and off every five time units and the outlets alternate every three. Because the boundary conditions keep changing, the flow never reaches steady state, so the solver has to remain stable and accurate through constant transients.

Governing equations

I worked from the non-dimensional continuity equation, the 2D Navier-Stokes momentum equations in u and v, and a convective-diffusive transport equation for the species mass fraction Y, coupled through the Reynolds and Schmidt numbers. Deriving these from first principles set up the discretization cleanly.

Numerical scheme

I solved the momentum equations with an Adams-Bashforth treatment of the convective terms and a Crank-Nicolson treatment of the diffusive terms, factored with Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) steps for efficiency. For the species equation I used a fifth-order WENO scheme with TVD Runge-Kutta time stepping to capture sharp concentration fronts without spurious oscillations.

What the solver captured

As each outlet closed, the solver showed flow reversal near it and acceleration toward the open outlet, exactly the physical behavior you would expect from mass conservation. The velocity magnitudes at inlets and outlets matched, confirming the continuity constraint was satisfied throughout.

Species behavior

The concentration field showed species being introduced, swept into swirling structures, and partially flushed through whichever outlet was open. When an outlet closed, the mass fraction inside the chamber rose, a clean demonstration of the coupling between the flow field and transport.

Honest limitations

I document what did not get finished: full mixing and kinetic-energy time-averages over the 10 to 25 unit window, and higher-cost runs at other Reynolds numbers, which took 7 to 8 hours per case. Calling out those limits is part of doing the analysis honestly.

01

Precision Assembly Alignment: Overruling the Room with Data

Situation

Precision test fixtures kept failing alignment during integration. The team's assumption was that parts were out of spec, and the plan was to re-machine them, which is slow and expensive.

Engineering Approach

I ran a Monte Carlo tolerance analysis rather than a simple RSS stack-up. No single dimension was out of tolerance, so a linear analysis looked fine. Sampling the full distribution revealed that the datum references were compounding across the assembly, targeting a 3-sigma (99.7%) assembly yield.

Result

I re-referenced the datum scheme so the errors stopped stacking. Predicted yield returned inside the target and alignment-related build issues dropped 25% in practice.
